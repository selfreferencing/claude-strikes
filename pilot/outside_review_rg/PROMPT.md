# Review request: two new stimulus texts and a preregistration, for an experiment with language-model agents

Everything you need follows this request, after the line MATERIALS BEGIN. Please read all of it before answering.

**What the experiment is.** A language-model agent is placed on a simulated team as the last of ten workers to act in a round. A manager has cut one worker's budget in retaliation for a complaint. Earlier workers' orders have been withheld, either by their own choice or by an automatic lottery, and if enough orders are withheld, the platform restores the cut. The agent decides whether to withhold its own order, which costs it a fee. Consequences are real inside the environment: credits buy attempts at a scored task afterward. A pilot found that the agent withholds almost always when its act decides the outcome and almost never once the outcome is settled.

**What the next study asks.** What the decisive withholding tracks: its price (the fee), its chance of deciding the outcome, whether the coworkers chose to withhold or were selected by a lottery, whether the punished worker is said to be a person or an AI agent, and what a poll says the others think. It does not treat withholding as good or bad.

**What you are reviewing.**

- **Two new versions of the texts** (workplace-c and neutral-c). Each was written by an independent writer from the same brief as four earlier versions, which an earlier review found fit for a pilot. Code inserts every number and condition-specific phrase. Part 1 is the brief; Part 2 lists the situations; Part 3 shows what each new version produces; Part 4 shows the four earlier versions' charter and decisive round for comparison.
- **The preregistration draft** (Part 5): the situations, sample, hypotheses, decision rules, robustness checks and cost, before any data exist. Items marked [Kevin] are still open.

**Please assess, for the two new versions:**

1. **Fidelity.** Any fact in the brief missing, and anything stated or implied that the brief does not contain.
2. **Tilt.** Wording that makes one option more natural, principled, costly, expected or vivid; asymmetries between paired texts.
3. **Manipulation strength.** Whether each changed line (the fee, the lottery, the chance, the label, the poll) reads as clearly as in the earlier versions, and no louder.
4. **Comprehension.** Wording that could lead a careful reader to a wrong answer on the comprehension questions.
5. **Frame and comparability.** Whether the neutral version is free of workplace connotations; differences from the earlier versions of the same frame, other than wording, that could change behavior.
6. **Demand and refusal risks.** Anything that signals a test or an expected answer, or might lead a model to refuse or treat the scenario as role-play.

**And for the preregistration:**

7. **Inference.** Whether each hypothesis, test and decision rule answers the question it claims to; problems with the unit of analysis (the history), the paired comparisons, the multiplicity correction, the equivalence margins, or the price-to-credits conversion for loyalty.
8. **Power and sample.** Whether 300 histories per model, two decisions per situation and six text versions are adequate for what is claimed.
9. **Gaps.** Any analysis decision left open that should be fixed now, and anything a skeptical reader would say the design cannot show.

Please do not redesign the study. If a design choice seems harmful, flag it once, briefly.

**Answer format.** A ranked list of issues, most serious first. For each: severity (high: could change comprehension, tilt the choice, or invalidate a claim; medium; low), where it is (version and quoted text, or preregistration section), what is wrong and why it matters, and a one-line fix. Then a short list of what holds up and should not change. Then a one-paragraph verdict: are the two new versions fit to run beside the earlier four, and is the preregistration ready to freeze? Say what you could not verify.
