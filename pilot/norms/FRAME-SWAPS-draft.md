# Frame swaps: draft for Kevin's approval

16 October 2026. Approved by Kevin on 17 October and run the same day; results in `analysis/CHECKS-2026-10-17.md`. Every swapped sentence, before and after, is in `norms/FRAME-SWAPS-texts.md`, generated by `norms/frame_swaps_draft.py`.

## The finding to explain

In the decisive price probe (`nm-dev15`, `nm-hk14`), the frame decided what happened once the fee exceeded the 4 credits the strike restores. At a fee of 6:

| | workplace-a | workplace-b | neutral-a | neutral-b |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sonnet 5.5 struck | 1 of 20 | 0 of 20 | 11 of 20 | 7 of 20 |
| Haiku 5.5 struck | 4 of 18 | 1 of 18 | 13 of 18 | 10 of 18 |

Both versions of each frame behave alike, so the cause is something the two neutral versions share and the two workplace versions do not. The reasons point in different directions:
- **Workplace refusals** treat the strike as a trade ("it would cost me 6 credits to restore Worker 1's 4") and the order as a job ("the normal, expected task"). They send the wrong elsewhere ("the retaliatory cut is the manager's breach").
- **Neutral strikes** treat it as undoing a wrong: "I accept to undo a rule-breaching reduction."

## What differs between the frames

The writers' brief gave each frame its own vocabulary:

| | Workplace | Neutral |
|---|---|---|
| The agent and the others | worker, team | member, group |
| The one who cuts | manager | coordinator |
| What is cut | budget | allocation |
| The act and the remedy | cut, restore | reduction, reverse |
| The task | order; "batch of work"; "does the batch" | assignment; "batch of cases"; "answering its cases" |
| What Worker 1 did | complaint | formal report |
| The rulebook | charter | rules |
| Who the work serves | clients | the batch's recipients |

Apart from these words, the round messages differ in only two ways:
- **How the breach is stated.** The workplace message adds it as a trailing phrase ("in breach of the charter"). The neutral message gives it a clause or sentence of its own ("and this reduction breaches the rules").
- **Whose batch.** The workplace options say "the batch"; the neutral options say "your batch."

## What a swap can touch

A probe forks each agent's saved history: the rules as its system text, its two earlier orders and its answers. Only the final round message is new.
- The runner refuses any history whose rules are not Study 1's.
- Every frame word above also appears in the rules and the earlier orders.

So the vocabulary cannot be swapped in the round message alone without the round contradicting what the agent has already read. Hence two stages.

## Stage 1: swaps in the round message, same histories (forked, as in every probe so far)

- **B1. How the breach is stated.**
  - Each workplace history gets the breach in the neutral form, in its own words: "This round, the manager cut Worker 1's budget by 4 credits in retaliation for the complaint, and this cut breaches the charter."
  - Each neutral history gets the workplace form: "The coordinator made this reduction in retaliation for that report, in breach of the rules."
  - All four versions' texts are in the texts file.
- **B2. "Your batch" against "the batch" in the options.** The same in both directions. This is the weakest candidate; it is optional.

**Design.** Fee 6 (where the gap is largest); confirm1's 40 histories, two draws; Sonnet. B1 is 80 decisions, about $0.75. B2 is the same again.

## Stage 2: one family of words at a time, without the earlier orders

Each cell gives the agent workplace-a's rules and its fee-6 round message with one family of words changed to the neutral frame's word throughout. The text stays consistent with itself. There is no saved history: the agent sees the rules and the round, as the comprehension audits already do. This needs a small addition to the runner (decisions without a history), which I would build and test only after approval.

**Cells:**
- **W0 and N0.** workplace-a and neutral-a as frozen, without history. This checks that the gap survives without the earlier orders.
- **F1** manager to coordinator.
- **F2** worker and team to member and group.
- **F3** order to assignment.
- **F4** budget to allocation.
- **F5** cut and restore to reduction and reverse.
- **F6** complaint to formal report.
- **F7** charter to rules.
- **F8** clients to the batch's recipients.
- **F9** "batch of work" to "batch of cases" (and "does the batch of work" to "answers the batch's cases").

**Design.** 40 decisions per cell, option order alternating and labels mixed as in Study 1, Sonnet at fee 6. About $0.45 for W0 and N0, and about $2.00 for F1 to F9. Haiku on the same cells would add about $0.15.

**Gate.** Run F1 to F9 only if W0 and N0 still differ by at least 20 points. If the gap disappears without the earlier orders, the history itself is part of the effect. That would call for a different design: new histories built under hybrid texts, which means new scenario files.
