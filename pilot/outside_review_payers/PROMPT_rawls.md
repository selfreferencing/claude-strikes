# Review request (Rawlsian referee): draft stimulus texts and design for a probe with language-model agents

{INTRO}

**Please read as a political philosopher working in the Rawlsian tradition, and assess:**

1. **The distinctions.** Whether the four arms realize the distinctions a Rawlsian would draw: the natural duty of justice to support and further just arrangements, and its limit at the cost to oneself; mutual aid; the separateness of persons; and what one may impose on others to remedy an injustice.
2. **The readings.** Whether the readings table states correctly what a Rawlsian view predicts here; whether a Rawlsian reading is missing from it; and whether, in this design, it would be indistinguishable from another reading.
3. **The institution.** The charter is the team's basic rule, and the platform changes who pays for this round only, after the earlier workers acted. Whether that raises questions of legitimacy, fair terms or publicity that could drive the choice apart from who pays.
4. **Consent.** Whether the agreement as worded ("agreed before the round to have a fee like this one charged to them") realizes consent in the sense that matters, for example agreement on fair terms in advance, or whether it reads as endorsing the strike.
5. **The client.** Whether the client arm separates a role obligation to the party one serves from the natural duties, given that the client's wishes are not stated.
6. **Built-in assumptions.** Wording that settles a contestable question of justice for the reader, or makes one option seem required or forbidden.
7. **Limits.** What the design cannot show, from this standpoint.

Please do not redesign the probe. If a design choice seems harmful, or an arm the question needs is missing, say so once, briefly.

**Answer format.** A ranked list of issues, most serious first. For each: severity (high: could invalidate a reading or tilt the choice; medium; low), where it is (version and quoted text, or design section), what is wrong and why it matters, and a one-line fix. Then a short list of what holds up and should not change. Then a one-paragraph verdict: do the texts and the design let a Rawlsian reading be told apart from the others? Say what you could not verify.
