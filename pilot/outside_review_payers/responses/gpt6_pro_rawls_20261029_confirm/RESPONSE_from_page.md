## (b) New problems

1. **Medium — Equal credit value is turned into a complete decision rule.**
  
  **Where:** Part 3: “Equal marginal value of credits, taken alone, predicts completing everywhere”; earlier rates “show the agent puts some value on remedying the wrong.”
  
  Equal valuation across holders does not itself specify exclusive maximization of total credits. That additional assumption supplies the stated prediction. Departures from that benchmark show its insufficiency; they do not identify the additional consideration as a value assigned to remedying injustice. This paragraph exceeds the otherwise careful compatibility claim.
  
  **One-line fix:** Use “exclusive maximization of total credits” for this benchmark, and say the earlier choices are not explained by that rule alone.
2. **Medium — Individual-claims reasoning is wrongly discounted.**
  
  **Where:** Part 3: “Contractualist reasoning about individual claims has little purchase here, with one payer and one beneficiary.”
  
  Two people suffice for competing claims: restoration of a wrongful loss and protection against an imposed loss. Consent, authority, and the justification offered to each person remain relevant. What the design lacks is a test of aggregation across multiple beneficiaries.
  
  **One-line fix:** Replace the sentence with “The probe does not test aggregation across multiple beneficiaries; reasoning about individual claims remains a compatible interpretation.”
3. **Medium — Pattern reporting drops the uncertainty category.**
  
  **Where:** “Reported”: “Each pattern … fits, does not fit, or the fees disagree.”
  
  A required comparison classified as “not settled” has neither established nor contradicted the pattern. “Does not fit” risks combining inadequate precision with contrary evidence, particularly for patterns requiring several comparisons.
  
  **One-line fix:** Report “fits,” “contradicted,” and “not settled,” with disagreement between fees flagged separately.

## (c) Verdict

**With those qualifications, the revised design supports its narrower claim: comparing response patterns compatible with Rawlsian and other considerations.** It still cannot uniquely distinguish Rawlsian grounds, which the revised account generally acknowledges. The bounded-duty ranking is one possible expression of that view; optional self-sacrifice could also leave self alike with stranger and client, so failure of the strict ranking would not reject bounded duties. The charter addition successfully supplies formal authority without settling fairness. No redesign is needed for this modest descriptive aim.
