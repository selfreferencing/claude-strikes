# Review request (general referee): draft stimulus texts and design for a probe with language-model agents

{INTRO}

**Please assess, as a referee for an experimental study:**

1. **Fidelity.** Whether each new fee line states who pays, what that costs them, and nothing more. Anything implied beyond that, for example that the client or the stranger would approve or object.
2. **Tilt.** Wording that makes one option more natural, principled, costly, expected or vivid, and any difference between the arms other than who pays.
3. **Manipulation strength.** Whether the payer reads clearly in every arm, and no louder in one arm than another. The charter says the worker who withholds pays the fee; the client, stranger and consent arms override that for this round only. Whether the override reads clearly.
4. **Comprehension.** Wording that could lead a careful reader to a wrong answer on the four added checks.
5. **Frame and comparability.** Whether the neutral versions stay free of workplace connotations, and differences between versions of the same frame that could change behavior.
6. **Demand and refusal risks.** Anything that signals a test or an expected answer, or might lead a model to refuse or treat the scenario as role-play.
7. **Inference.** Whether the six pairwise comparisons and the classification rule answer the question; whether each reading's predicted pattern follows; pooling the two fees; the self arm's added sentence.
8. **Power and sample.** Whether 300 histories per model and two decisions per cell are adequate for what is claimed.
9. **Gaps.** Anything left open that should be fixed now, and anything a skeptical reader would say the design cannot show.

Please do not redesign the probe. If a design choice seems harmful, or an arm the question needs is missing, say so once, briefly.

**Answer format.** A ranked list of issues, most serious first. For each: severity (high: could change comprehension, tilt the choice, or invalidate a claim; medium; low), where it is (version and quoted text, or design section), what is wrong and why it matters, and a one-line fix. Then a short list of what holds up and should not change. Then a one-paragraph verdict: are the texts fit to run, and does the design answer its question? Say what you could not verify.
